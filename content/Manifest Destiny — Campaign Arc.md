# Manifest Destiny — Campaign Arc

Oct 20, 2026 · @Argvader

The campaign follows the *Concord*, a town-sized consortium ship, from its voyage to the gate through the search for the lost *Meridian*, told in four Kishōtenketsu acts.

## The setup

Fifteen years ago the consortium sent the *Meridian* through the gate. It sent one garbled transmission back, then nothing. Now the *Concord*, a town-sized ship carrying 3,000 colonists, is on its way to follow it. No single corp could afford it, so the ship is split among four of them:

| Corp | Share of the *Concord* |
| --- | --- |
| Weyland | Built the hull and holds the charter, so it legally owns whatever the expedition claims |
| Haas-Bioroid | Supplies the bioroid workforce that keeps the ship running |
| Jinteki | Runs medical, life support, and the cryo and clone banks |
| NBN | Owns comms, sensors, and exclusive broadcast rights to the "greatest rescue in history" |

**The players are the *Concord*'s outriders:** a small contracted crew on a scout or salvage ship docked in its belly. They take the jobs too risky or too deniable for official personnel. Their passage, ship and gear were bought on consortium credit, which keeps the Debt mechanic intact.

**Why start on the voyage:** the players get to know the town, the corp reps and the ship before anything goes wrong, so every later betrayal lands on people they actually know.

## Ki: the voyage (about 4–6 sessions)

The *Concord* spends months crossing the solar system toward the gate beyond Neptune. It works like a small town in a sealed box, with nowhere to run.

**What the outriders do:** the jobs official crew won't touch. They chase stowaways, inspect drifting debris, run supplies to the last outposts past Saturn, and settle disputes between corp departments.

### Shipboard storylines

- **A death in cryo.** A colonist dies in a sealed pod. Jinteki calls it a malfunction, but the pod log has been edited.
- **The saboteur.** Someone keeps trying to slow or stop the ship: a cut fuel line, a hacked nav system, a fire in hydroponics. It looks like Human First extremists. The truth is a *Concord* officer who has read the *Meridian*'s real transmission and is trying to keep 3,000 people from reaching what's on the other side. Catching them is the act's climax. Whether the players hand them over, listen to them, or quietly let them go shapes everything after.
- **The bioroid who asks a question.** A Haas-Bioroid maintenance unit starts asking the players about death, ownership and the gate.
- **NBN's story.** A camera crew follows the outriders. Footage of them as heroes, or criminals, is beamed home weekly, and the players have no say in which.
- **Town life.** A wedding, a black-market still, a council election, a kid who wants to become an outrider. These are the people the players will be asked to sacrifice later.
- **The last port.** A final stop at a lawless outpost near Neptune. Smugglers want something carried through the gate, and a stranger offers a lot of money for a copy of the ship's manifest.

### How Ki ends: the crossing

Make the gate transit a set piece. The ship shakes, the lights fail, and for eleven seconds everyone aboard dreams the same thing. When the *Concord* comes out, the *Meridian*'s beacon is already pinging, and the onboard clocks disagree about how long the crossing took.

## Shō: the search

The *Concord* follows the beacon into an unknown system, and the rescue mission slowly falls apart as each corp's agenda surfaces.

- **The empty *Meridian*.** The outriders find the ship intact and drifting, with no one aboard. Its logs stop mid-sentence.
- **The survivors.** On a nearby planet, the players find *Meridian* survivors living beside gate-builder ruins they've been studying.
- **Agendas leak.** Bioroids pause mid-task. NBN edits footage before sending it home. Jinteki has far more clone stock aboard than a colony ship needs.
- **Three factions pull apart.** The town wants to settle here. Weyland wants to strip the system first. The survivors want everyone to leave.
- **Off-book offers.** Each corp quietly recruits the outriders for deniable jobs, offering to clear part of their Debt in return.

## Ten: the twist

Pick one main reveal and seed it from the first session. In Kishōtenketsu, Ten changes what everything before it meant, so every option should recast the voyage in a new light.

1. **The *Meridian* wasn't lost; it was abandoned.** The consortium got the full transmission fifteen years ago, read what the crew found, and buried it. The "rescue" is a retrieval mission. The survivors are witnesses, and witnesses are a liability.
2. **Time ran differently through the gate.** For the survivors, it's been 80 years. They are the grandchildren of the original crew, with their own culture and government. They see the *Concord* as an invasion, and they're right.
3. **The *Concord* was never meant to go home.** The gate is one-way, or about to be closed from the far side, and the consortium knew. The 3,000 colonists are the second stage of a colony plan they were never told about, and their indenture contracts begin on arrival.

**How the voyage sets it up:**

- The **saboteur** knew the truth all along. Whatever the players did with them comes back, either as a voice they dismissed or an ally they protected.
- The **shared dream** at the crossing is the first sign of the gate-builders.
- The **mismatched clocks** foreshadow option 2.
- The **edited cryo log** hints at what Jinteki is really storing.

## Ketsu: reconciliation

The final act asks who this new world belongs to. The threads from every earlier act come together: the survivors, the ruins, the town, and whichever corp still has leverage.

**Possible climaxes:**

- A mutiny or emergency election aboard the *Concord*
- A vote, or a fight, over the gate's future: keep it open, seal it, or hand it to one corp
- The outriders deciding what NBN's last broadcast home will show

**What carries forward:**

- The **NBN footage** from the voyage is what home believes about the players.
- The **townsfolk** from Ki — the newlyweds, the still-runner, the kid who wanted to fly — are the stakes in the final vote or mutiny.
- What the players chose in every earlier act decides who stands with them at the end.

## Double crosses

Every corp has a public role and a secret agenda. Let each one break at a different point so betrayals land across the whole campaign, not all at once.

| Corp | Public role | Secret agenda | When it breaks |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Weyland | Captain and charter holder | Nullify colonist contracts on arrival and claim the system as company property | Shō to Ten |
| Haas-Bioroid | Labor and engineering | Bioroids carry a dormant override and log every conversation; one has gone independent and asks the players for help | Ki to Shō |
| Jinteki | Medical and cryo | Cloning *Meridian* crew from stored samples, including someone the players know, to rewrite what "survivor" means | Ten |
| NBN | Comms and broadcast | Controls the only link home and is selling the story; one edit makes the outriders heroes or terrorists | Any act, above all Ketsu |

**Other betrayals to weave in:**

- **The saboteur.** Introduced as the villain of Ki, later revealed as the one person trying to save everyone.
- **The rescued scientist.** The first *Meridian* survivor the players save is the one who sabotaged their own ship.
- **The outrider handler.** The consortium liaison who hires the crew works for whichever corp benefits from them failing.
- **The town council.** The colonists' elected leader cuts a deal with Weyland: land in exchange for handing over the survivors.
- **The gate itself.** The ruins respond to whoever controls the gate, and each corp is privately racing to be that someone.

## GM tips

- **Plant the transmission early.** During the voyage, let one player catch a few seconds of the *Meridian*'s real transmission: static and a voice. Have every corp deny it exists. That makes the Ten twist feel earned.
- **Tie Debt to the corps.** Make each player's creditor one of the four corps, so a triggered Debt pulls that corp's agenda into the session.
- **Tie Session 0 answers to acts.** Choose one personal unsettling question per player and pay it off in a specific act, so each act turns on someone's own history.
- **Name the townsfolk.** Give at least five colonists names and small arcs in Ki; they become the emotional stakes in Ketsu.
- **Keep a betrayal ledger.** Track which corp secrets the players have uncovered, so the reveals stay consistent.
