# Manifest Destiny — Session 0

Oct 19, 2026 · @Argvader

## Session 0 at a glance

By the end of the night the table should have a crew, a ship, a debt that binds them, and a voyage they helped invent. Plan on about four hours with a break.

| Time | Block | Output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00 – 0:15 | Pitch and tone dials | Shared picture of the campaign |
| 0:15 – 0:35 | Safety tools, lines and veils | Agreed lines and veils list |
| 0:35 – 1:30 | Character concepts and stats | Archetype, career, rough build |
| 1:30 – 1:55 | Unsettling questions (personal, crew, motive) | Backstory hooks, crew bonds |
| 1:55 – 2:05 | Break |  |

**Bring:** Genesys Core Rulebook, *Shadow of the Beanstalk*, *Expanded Player's Guide*, *Shattered Empire*, narrative dice, index cards, a d20 for each player.

## Pitch and tone

Open with the read-aloud, then set the dials together so nobody is surprised by the campaign they signed up for.

> Fifteen years ago, the *Meridian* went through the gate beyond Neptune, the only ship that ever has. It sent home one transmission, a burst of static and a voice nobody could make out, and then nothing.
>
> The corporations said a rescue was impossible. Then NBN made the families' grief into the most-watched story in human history, and suddenly it was a business opportunity. No single corp could afford a ship big enough, so four of them split the bill. Weyland built the hull and holds the charter. Haas-Bioroid supplied the bioroids who run it. Jinteki keeps the colonists alive. NBN owns every word and every image that goes home. They signed a compact promising to share the cost, the credit and whatever waits on the other side. None of them believes the others will keep it.
>
> The *Concord* is a town in a hull: 3,000 colonists, a council, a chapel, a black market, a school. You don't fly it. The Compact forbids the corps from fielding their own scouts, so it opened two outrider berths in the *Concord*'s belly to outside bids. Hundreds of crews bid, with ships bought on corporate sponsorships, family fortunes, crowdfunding campaigns and money nobody asks about. Two won. One is the *Yarrow*, the crowdfunded darling of NBN's broadcast. The other is yours. You take the jobs too risky or too deniable for anyone on the official roster, and in return the Compact promises outriders a stake in whatever the expedition finds. You owe someone for the privilege, and so does everyone aboard.
>
> Every day of the voyage, home gets a little further away. Messages take minutes, then hours, then longer. Somewhere past Neptune the corps will stop being able to ask the Core what to do, and they will have to decide for themselves, aboard a ship where nobody can walk away.
>
> Out there, past the last beacon, something built a door. The *Meridian* walked through it. You're going to find out why it never came back.
>
> Down in the *Concord*'s belly, someone is banging on your hatch.

The full setting briefing (New Angeles, archetypes, the outrider bid and the Quorum) is in *Manifest Destiny — Session 0 Briefing*.

**Touchstones:** *Firefly*, *The Expanse*, *Alien*, *Deadwood*, *Outland*, the Android: Netrunner card art.

**Tone dials** (have each player put a mark on a 1–5 scale, then settle on the table's number):

| Dial | 1 | 5 | Default |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Grit | Swashbuckling caper | Every bullet and credit counts | 4 |
| Corporate cruelty | Cartoon villains | Banal, believable atrocity | 3 |
| Alien weirdness | Background flavor | Cosmic horror front and center | 2, rising |
| Crew conflict | Found family | Knives out, secrets kept | 2 |
| Moral lines | Clear heroes | Everyone is compromised | 4 |

**What a session looks like:** a job, a complication, a choice about who gets hurt, and a payday that is never quite enough. The gate-builder mystery is a slow burn in the background, not the weekly plot.

## Safety and table expectations

Use the X-card plus open-door play, and fill in lines and veils before anyone answers an unsettling question. The questions below are designed to sting a little; players can always pass, answer vaguely, or ask to come back to one.

**Themes this campaign is likely to touch** (go down the list and sort each into line, veil or fine):

- Indentured labor and debt bondage
- Bioroids and clones treated as property; questions of personhood
- Body modification, cybernetic replacement, losing parts of yourself
- Corporate violence against colonists, including civilians and children
- Isolation, vacuum death, being stranded
- Alien body horror and parasitism
- Addiction (stims, combat drugs)
- Betrayal inside the crew

**Table expectations to agree on:**

- PvP: secrets between characters are fine; killing or robbing another PC needs that player's OK.
- Character death: possible but telegraphed. Despair and critical injuries hurt; a death will never come from a single unlucky roll without warning.
- The crew stays together. Give your character a reason to keep flying with these people, even if they don't like them.
- Spotlight: the GM will rotate jobs so each player's skills and debt come up.
- Scheduling, snacks, phones, and what happens when someone misses a session.

## Character creation

Build with the *Shadow of the Beanstalk* archetypes and careers, then pick a frontier role so the crew covers the jobs a ship needs. Concepts first, numbers second.

**Frontier roles** (aim for at least four covered across the crew):

| Role | What they do on a job | Skills to lean on |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pilot | Flies the ship, runs blockades, lands where you shouldn't | Piloting, Gunnery, Cool |
| Mechanic | Keeps a wreck flying, strips salvage | Mechanics, Computers, Vigilance |
| Face | Negotiates jobs, talks past checkpoints | Charm, Deception, Negotiation |
| Gun | Protects the crew, collects bounties | Ranged, Brawl, Perception |
| Runner | Hacks corp systems and sensor nets | Computers, Stealth, Knowledge |
| Doc | Patches bodies and bioroid chassis | Medicine, Mechanics, Resilience |
| Prospector | Reads planets, ruins and ore fields | Survival, Knowledge, Perception |

**Archetype notes for this campaign:**

- **Clones** and **bioroids** are legally property in the Core. Out here, papers can be forged, but someone may still hold the title.
- **G-mods** and **cyborgs** often got their upgrades on credit. That's an easy Debt.
- **Natural humans** may have the cleanest records and the most to lose by flying with this crew.

**Corp favors:** for examples of what a corp did for a character (and now holds over them), see *Corp favors for character creation* in the Session 0 Briefing.

**Hard rules for builds:** no corp loyalists unless their loyalty is already cracking, and every character must have a reason they can't just go home to the Core.

## Unsettling questions

Each player rolls a d20 and answers that personal question, rolling again if the number's taken, then answers one crew question about the player on their left. Answers become canon and the GM's hooks. Keep answers to a few sentences; leave gaps on purpose.

**How to run it:** go around the table. After each answer, any one player may ask a single follow-up, and the answering player may decline it. The GM writes every name, place and object mentioned on an index card.

### Personal questions (roll a d20)

1. You've seen the inside of a corporate contract-labor camp. What number did they tattoo, stamp or chip into you, and why haven't you had it removed?
2. Since the Concord left port, you've heard something on an empty comms channel. What did it say, and why did it use your name?
3. You once left someone behind in vacuum to save yourself. Who were they, and what do you tell people happened?
4. Which corporation owns something that used to be part of you: a limb, a memory, a patent, a face?
5. What did the consortium promise you when you signed on to the Concord, and when did you realize it was a lie?
6. You keep a small object from a colony that no longer exists. What is it, and who erased the colony?
7. What do you do with your hands when the ship is quiet, and what are you trying not to think about?
8. Someone back in the Core still holds a funeral for you every year. Why do they think you're dead?
9. You signed something you didn't read. What did it turn out to say?
10. When you look at a bioroid, what do you feel that you'd never say out loud?
11. You've held a sliver of the gate itself, smuggled off a Weyland survey. What has been different about you since?
12. Who is the last person who trusted you completely, and what happened to them?
13. Weyland holds the charter, so everything the expedition claims is legally theirs. What have you kept off the Concord's manifest so they can never own it?
14. You worked a Haas-Bioroid line, beside the bioroids or as one of them. What did you see there that your contract forbids you to describe?
15. Someone you love is asleep in a Jinteki cryo pod aboard the Concord. Who are they, and what do you owe Jinteki to keep them there?
16. NBN interviewed you for the "greatest rescue in history" about someone you lost on the Meridian. Who was it, and what did you refuse to say on camera?
17. Weyland security keeps a file on you with one line blacked out. What's under it, and who ordered it struck?
18. The consortium should never have cleared you to fly as an outrider. Who signed off anyway, and what did they want for it?
19. One of the 3,000 colonists recognized you on the first day. Who are they, what do they know, and why haven't they told anyone?
20. Human First reached out to you before the Concord left port. What did they ask of you, and what did you tell them?

### Crew questions (about the player on your left)

1. What did you see them do on their first job with this crew that you still haven't asked about?
2. Which of their habits makes you sure they're hiding something?
3. They saved your life once. What did it cost them, and have you paid it back?
4. You've heard them talking in their sleep. What name do they say?
5. If a corp offered you a bounty on them, what is the number where you'd hesitate?
6. What do they cook, play or sing that makes the ship feel like home for an hour?
7. On launch day NBN's cameras caught them doing something they asked you to keep off the feed. What was it?
8. They're the reason the crew won the bid, or nearly lost it. What did they do?
9. What do they tell the *Yarrow*'s crew that they've never told you?
10. Which corp delegate or officer aboard the *Concord* do they go quiet around, and what do you think happened between them?
11. They've promised to get one of the colonists through this voyage alive. Who, and why do they care?
12. What on the ship will they never let anyone else touch?

### Bid questions (as a crew, after building the ship)

Run these in their own block once the ship is built: the backer paid for the ship, and both the backer and the berth fee feed the crew's Debt. Record the backer, the stake and the promise on the manager's *The bid* tab.

The *Concord* carries two outrider crews, each of which won its berth by open bid: yours, and the crowdfunded *Yarrow*. Pick a backer for the crew's ship (corporate sponsor through a front, crowdfunding, family money, colonial co-op, patron, orgcrime, or self-funded), then answer together:

1. Who backed your bid, and what did they pay for?
2. What did your crew promise in the bid that you aren't sure you can deliver?
3. Which one stake did your crew bid for, and what did you give up to get it?
4. What happened the first time your crew met the *Yarrow*'s, and why has NBN never aired the footage?
5. A crew lost the bid by a single vote and isn't aboard. Who were they, and why do they think you cheated?

### Why you're here (each player picks one)

The outrider contract promises one stake on top of pay, and the crew bid for it together: finder's share, homestead grant, salvage rights, broadcast points, debt amnesty, or a seat on the first ship home. Each player's own reason for coming may not match it.

1. The crew bid for one stake. What are *you* really here for, and what would you trade the crew's stake to get it?
2. Someone you know backed the bid with their own money. Who, and what happens to them if you fail?
3. You could have signed on as a colonist and gone safely. Why did you choose to fly as an outrider instead?
4. Someone tried to buy your place on the crew before launch. Who, and how much did they offer?
5. The backer asked one of you to report on the others privately. If that was you, what did you say? If it wasn't, who do you suspect?

### World questions (players define the road to the gate)

1. Name the lawless outpost near Neptune that's the Concord's last port before the gate. What rule is never broken there?
2. What does Dana Quill, the consortium liaison who holds the crew's Debt, keep in a jar on the desk?
3. Describe a dead station the Concord passes on the way out. What's still waiting inside?
4. Name a Free Claims outpost past Saturn where the crew has drunk. What did the corps do to the last one nearby?
5. What do spacers leave on a derelict before stripping it, and what happens to people who forget?
6. Name a rumor about the gate-builders that everyone aboard the Concord has heard. Which part is true? (Only the GM knows.)
7. You've watched a Quorum session from the gallery. Which delegate was the only one who seemed to be telling the truth?
8. What did the colonists nickname the Quorum, and which delegate hates that name most?
9. Who is the council speaker, and why did the colonists elect someone the corps can't stand?

## Building the ship

The crew builds one ship together: pick a base hull from *Shattered Empire*, customize it with the *Expanded Player's Guide* rules, then give it a history. The ship should feel like a character with its own debts and ghosts.

1. **Choose a hull.** A light freighter or salvage tug is the default. Agree on silhouette, handling and crew size together.
2. **Spend hard points.** Each player picks one attachment that reflects their role (smuggling compartments, a medbay, a sensor suite, an extra turret).
3. **Pick two quirks.** One helpful, one troublesome. Examples: the airlock opens a half-second late; the nav computer occasionally plots courses to coordinates nobody entered; the galley stove only works if you hit it.
4. **Name it.** The name painted on the hull and the name on the registry should be different.
5. **Answer the ship's questions** below, one per player.

### The ship's unsettling questions

1. Who owned this ship before you, and where did you find their last log entry?
2. There's a sealed compartment none of you has opened. What do you hear from it during long burns?
3. What corporation's paint is still visible under the current coat?
4. Someone died aboard. Where, and what does the crew avoid doing in that spot?
5. The nav computer holds a route to the gate that nobody aboard entered. Who plotted it, and how do you know?
6. What part of the ship was salvaged from a vessel that was destroyed with all hands?

**Ship debt:** the berth fee and passage are on consortium credit; the ship itself came from whoever backed the crew's bid. Note who holds the title; both feed the crew's starting Debt below.

## Debt

Debt works like *Edge of the Empire*'s Obligation: each character owes someone, the crew's values add into one d100 range, and at the start of each session the GM rolls to see whose debt comes calling. A triggered debt drains the crew's focus (lower strain thresholds that session) and pulls that character's creditor into the plot.

**Starting values (house adaptation):** 4–5 players start at 10 Debt each; 3 players at 15; 2 players at 20. Any player may take +5 or +10 more for extra starting XP or credits. Players can pay Debt down with job earnings or favors, and the GM can add it when they cut deals.

**Debt types** (pick one, then name the creditor):

| Type | Example creditor | How it comes due |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Contract | Haas-Bioroid indenture office | Repo agents, legal seizure |
| Loan | Drift crime boss | A job you can't refuse |
| Title | Jinteki clone registry | Someone claims you as property |
| Bounty | Argus Security | Hunters arrive at the worst moment |
| Favor | A Free Claims colony elder | They need you, now |
| Blood | Family of someone you got killed | Revenge |
| Addiction | A stim dealer on the Bone Fields | Withdrawal, or a supplier wanting payment in kind |
| Secret | NBN investigative stringer | Exposure |

**Debt question for every player:** your creditor has something of yours as collateral. What is it, and what would they do with it if you stopped paying?

## Wrap-up and GM follow-up

End by reading back the crew's debts and the ship's name, then tease the first job: a salvage contract on a derelict that matches one player's answer.
