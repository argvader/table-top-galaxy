# Manifest Destiny — Session 0 Briefing

Nov 3, 2026 · @Argvader

Setting material to share at Session 0: where everyone comes from, who they might be, the read-aloud that opens the campaign, why the outriders are aboard, and how the *Concord* governs itself once home stops answering. Everything above the **GM only** marker is safe to read or hand to players.

## New Angeles

New Angeles sprawls along the equator at the foot of the Beanstalk, the space elevator that made the city the busiest port in human space. It is a wall of arcologies and neon a hundred kilometers long, where the rich live high in the towers and the poor live in the Root, the cramped districts beneath the Beanstalk's base. Weyland built the Beanstalk, NBN broadcasts every second of the city's life, Haas-Bioroid's androids sweep its streets and staff its factories, and Jinteki's clones work the jobs nobody else will take. Every cargo pod that climbs the Beanstalk to orbit, and every ship that leaves from there for Mars, the Moon or the outer system, starts here. So does the *Concord*. Most of its 3,000 colonists are New Angelenos who signed contracts in a crowded hiring hall in the Root. They are betting that whatever lies past the gate has to be better than a city where the corps own the air.

## Archetypes

- **Natural.** An unmodified human, born the old-fashioned way. You aren't special, which is exactly why the corps underestimate you, and why the *Concord*'s colonists trust you before anyone else.
- **G-Mod.** Your genes were edited before you were born, by parents who could afford it or a corp that paid for it. You're better than baseline, and someone may still think they own the patent on you.
- **Cyborg.** Part of you is machine, usually because the original was lost on a job. The parts were expensive, the payments are ongoing, and the warranty doesn't cover what happens past Neptune.
- **Loonie.** You were born in Heinlein on the Moon, where you learned to live in low gravity and to make do with scraps. The Earthers on the *Concord* think you're odd; you think they don't know how to live in a sealed box yet.
- **Clone.** Jinteki grew you in a vat for a purpose and conditioned you to serve it. In the Core you're legally property. Aboard a ship where Jinteki runs medical, your owner is never far away.
- **Bioroid.** A Haas-Bioroid android with a human-mapped mind, built to work and governed by laws you cannot break. The *Concord* runs on bioroid labor, so every one of your kind aboard is watching to see what happens to you.

## Opening read-aloud

Read this before the tone dials. It works whether the first session opens on launch day or weeks into the voyage; pick the closing line that fits.

> Fifteen years ago, the *Meridian* went through the gate beyond Neptune, the only ship that ever has. It sent home one transmission, a burst of static and a voice nobody could make out, and then nothing.
>
> The corporations said a rescue was impossible. Then NBN made the families' grief into the most-watched story in human history, and suddenly it was a business opportunity. No single corp could afford a ship big enough, so four of them split the bill. Weyland built the hull and holds the charter. Haas-Bioroid supplied the bioroids who run it. Jinteki keeps the colonists alive. NBN owns every word and every image that goes home. They signed a compact promising to share the cost, the credit and whatever waits on the other side. None of them believes the others will keep it.
>
> The *Concord* is a town in a hull: 3,000 colonists, a council, a chapel, a black market, a school. You don't fly it. The Compact forbids the corps from fielding their own scouts, so it opened two outrider berths in the *Concord*'s belly to outside bids. Hundreds of crews bid, with ships bought on corporate sponsorships, family fortunes, crowdfunding campaigns and money nobody asks about. Two won. One is the *Yarrow*, the crowdfunded darling of NBN's broadcast. The other is yours. You take the jobs too risky or too deniable for anyone on the official roster, and in return the Compact promises outriders a stake in whatever the expedition finds. You owe someone for the privilege, and so does everyone aboard.
>
> Every day of the voyage, home gets a little further away. Messages take minutes, then hours, then longer. Somewhere past Neptune the corps will stop being able to ask the Core what to do, and they will have to decide for themselves, aboard a ship where nobody can walk away.
>
> Out there, past the last beacon, something built a door. The *Meridian* walked through it.
>
> You're going to find out why it never came back.

**Closing line if you start before launch:** *"The countdown on the Broadcast Square screens reads six hours. Down in the Concord's belly, someone is banging on your hatch."*

**Closing line if you start after launch:** *"Earth is a blue dot behind you now. Down in the Concord's belly, someone is banging on your hatch."*

## The outriders

This is what every character knows. NBN covered the bidding like a sports draft.

**Why outriders exist.** None of the four corps trusts the others to run the *Concord*'s scouting, salvage or investigations, so the Compact bars them from doing it themselves. Instead it sold two outrider berths to independent crews by open bid. Outriders are contractors, not crew. They answer to the Quorum, take jobs from anyone aboard who can pay, and keep their own ship docked in the *Concord*'s hangar.

**Two crews.** The players' ship is one of two. The other is the *Yarrow*, named for the herb field medics pack to stop bleeding. Its crew was crowdfunded by the Bring Them Home campaign, which *Meridian* families started after NBN's feature on the lost ship. Its captain, Tamsin Reyes, lost her sister on the *Meridian* and is on NBN's feeds every week. The two crews compete for the same jobs, the same Findings and the same stakes, and are sometimes each other's only help within a light-hour.

**The bid.** Each crew paid a berth fee and put forward a ship. The berth fee, passage and supplies are carried on consortium credit, so every outrider starts aboard in debt to the consortium. The ship itself came from whoever backed the bid.

| Backer | What it looks like | What they expect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Corporate sponsor | A corp or subsidiary paid for the ship through a contractor or holding company, since the Compact bars them from bidding directly | Loyalty, information, and a crew that does what it's told when it counts |
| Crowdfunding | Thousands of strangers chipped in after an NBN feature or a viral pitch | Updates, footage, and a story worth their money; they can turn on you in a day |
| Family money | An old family, a dynasty, or one rich relative | A return, a reputation restored, or a black sheep sent far away |
| Colonial co-op | A Free Claims colony or a workers' collective pooled its savings | That you look after their people and their interests out there |
| Patron | One wealthy individual with private reasons | Something specific, which they haven't fully explained |
| Orgcrime | A syndicate, laundered through enough shells to pass inspection | A cut of everything, and favors on demand |
| Self-funded | The crew sold everything they owned, or stole it | Nobody, which means nobody covers you either |

**What outriders get.** The outrider contract lists stakes a crew can earn on top of its pay. Each crew bid for one stake, which it shares: nobody collects unless the crew completes the contract together. Each player also has a personal reason for coming, which may not match the crew's stake.

| Stake | What the contract says |
| --- | --- |
| Finder's share | A percentage of the assessed value of anything the crew is first to log: resources, technology, ruins |
| Homestead grant | First choice of land claims on any habitable world the expedition charters |
| Salvage rights | A share of anything recovered from the *Meridian* or other wrecks the crew reaches first |
| Broadcast points | Royalty points on NBN footage and the eventual feature about the rescue |
| Debt amnesty | Consortium debt retired in full when the expedition concludes |
| A seat on the first ship home | Guaranteed passage back to the Core |

### Outrider questions (Session 0)

Answer these as a crew during ship building, then each player answers one personal question.

**Crew questions:**

1. Who backed your bid, and what did they pay for? (Pick from the backer table, then name them.)
2. What did your crew promise in the bid that you aren't sure you can deliver?
3. Which one stake did your crew bid for, and what did you give up to get it?
4. What happened the first time your crew met the *Yarrow*'s, and why has NBN never aired the footage?
5. A crew lost the bid by a single vote and isn't aboard. Who were they, and why do they think you cheated?

**Why you're here (each player picks one):**

1. The crew bid for one stake. What are *you* really here for, and what would you trade the crew's stake to get it?
2. Someone you know backed the bid with their own money. Who, and what happens to them if you fail?
3. You could have signed on as a colonist and gone safely. Why did you choose to fly as an outrider instead?
4. Someone tried to buy your place on the crew before launch. Who, and how much did they offer?
5. The backer asked one of you to report on the others privately. If that was you, what did you say? If it wasn't, who do you suspect?

## The *Concord* Compact: who decides

This is what every character knows. It is printed in the colonists' orientation packet, and NBN ran an explainer on it.

The four corps govern the *Concord* through the **Quorum**, a board of four executive delegates who sit at one table on the command deck. The ship's captain is a Weyland officer and runs the ship day to day, but the Quorum sets policy, settles disputes between departments and decides where the *Concord* goes.

**Standing.** Each delegate votes with their corp's Standing, a number in the Compact that measures its stake in the expedition. A motion passes with more than half of all Standing. The Compact recalculates Standing at milestones, based on what each corp has contributed and what it has cost the voyage. In practice, every corp can gain or lose power on the way to the gate.

| Corp | Starting Standing | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weyland | 4 | Hull, charter and captain |
| Haas-Bioroid | 2 | Workforce and engineering |
| Jinteki | 2 | Medical, life support, cryo |
| NBN | 2 | Comms, sensors, funding from broadcast rights |

At the start, Weyland needs one partner to pass anything, and the other three can outvote Weyland together. Any corp that reaches 6 Standing rules alone, and every delegate knows it.

**Distance.** The Compact hands authority from the Core to the Quorum in stages, as the light-lag home gets longer:

| Band | Where | Message lag (one way) | Who decides |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Tether** | Earth to Jupiter | Seconds to about 45 minutes | The consortium boards in the Core. The Quorum advises and carries out orders. |
| **Long Line** | Saturn to Neptune | About 1½ to 4 hours | The Quorum decides. Any home board can veto a decision within 72 hours of receiving it. |
| **Dark** | The gate and beyond | Unknown, possibly none | The Quorum is sovereign. Its decisions can't be appealed. |

**Ties.** In the Tether and Long Line bands, a tied vote is referred home. In the Dark, the captain breaks ties.

**The council seat.** The colonists elect a town council, and its speaker sits at the Quorum table with a voice but no Standing. The speaker can speak on any motion and vote on none.

**Findings.** When departments dispute the facts (whose fault the fire was, who edited a log, who owns a piece of salvage), the Quorum can commission an independent Finding. Officially, a Finding comes from a neutral party. In practice, that means the outriders.

### Session 0 world questions for the Quorum

These are world questions 7–9 in the Session 0 doc and the campaign manager:

1. You've watched a Quorum session from the gallery. Which delegate was the only one who seemed to be telling the truth?
2. What did the colonists nickname the Quorum, and which delegate hates that name most?
3. Who is the council speaker, and why did the colonists elect someone the corps can't stand?

## GM only

### Why this works as an arc

The Compact turns the long voyage into a ticking clock. In the Tether, nobody can grab power, because the boards at home will stop them. In the Long Line, a corp can act and hope the veto comes too late. In the Dark, whoever holds the most Standing when the *Concord* crosses decides everything that happens in Shō. Every corp is quietly trying to arrive at the gate holding the most Standing, and the outriders' Findings and side jobs are one of the main ways Standing moves.

It also sets up Ketsu. The final vote or mutiny is a fight over who sits at that table, and the council seat with a voice but no vote is the obvious fault line.

### The truth about the outrider berths

The Compact bars corps from fielding scouts, so every corp quietly tried to buy a berth through a front. The bid was decided by the Quorum, and it was rigged in every direction at once. The outriders are proxies in the Standing game: whoever controls the crews controls who discovers what, and what the Findings say.

**The *Yarrow*.** The crowdfunding is real money from real families, but NBN seeded the Bring Them Home campaign through a subsidiary, matched every donation, and owns the crew's image rights. NBN writes the *Yarrow* better lines, better scenes and, if needed, a better story than the players get. Captain Reyes believes the campaign is what it says it is. Finding out it isn't, and what NBN cut from her sister's story, is her arc.

**Alternate names** if *Yarrow* doesn't land: the *Meridian's Wake*, the *Lantern* (for the light left in the window), the *Kept Promise*, the *Second Meridian*, or the *Tourniquet*. The campaign can be the *Meridian* Families Fund, Bring Them Home, or Light the Way.

**The players' crew is the swing vote.** Weyland, Haas-Bioroid and Jinteki all lost their fronts in the bid, so each wants a hold on the players. Whatever backer the players choose, decide secretly whether a corp is hiding behind it (Haas-Bioroid and Jinteki are the obvious candidates; Weyland already has the captain). If the players chose self-funded or family money, the corp is instead the one holding their consortium credit note. Reveal it when that player's Debt triggers or at the Ki-to-Shō break, when Haas-Bioroid's agenda cracks.

**Tie the backer to Debt.** Treat the backer as an extra creditor for the crew's shared Debt, alongside the consortium. A triggered Debt can bring the backer's demand to the hangar door.

### The fine print on the stakes

Every stake is real on paper and hollow in a way that ties to the Ten twists. Reveal the catch when the players try to collect.

| Stake | The catch |
| --- | --- |
| Finder's share | Payable on return to the Core. If the *Concord* was never meant to go home (twist 3), it is worthless. |
| Homestead grant | Issued under the colonists' charter, which Weyland plans to nullify on arrival. |
| Salvage rights | Exclude "proprietary consortium materials," which turns out to mean everything aboard the *Meridian* worth having. |
| Broadcast points | Calculated on NBN's net profit, which by NBN's accounting will always be zero. |
| Debt amnesty | Takes effect when the expedition is "concluded," as the Quorum defines it. The Quorum never will. |
| A seat on the first ship home | Nobody has said when the first ship home leaves, or whether one ever will. |

The crew's stake breaks in Ten, when its fine print lands on the whole table at once. Each player's personal reason is paid off separately, in its own act, so every act also turns on someone's own story. Watch for players whose reason doesn't match the crew's stake: they're the ones the corps will try to buy in Shō.

### Moving Standing

Track Standing on the betrayal ledger. Adjust it at milestones (the end of each band, a major disaster, the crossing), not after every scene, so changes feel earned:

| Event | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| A Finding blames a corp's department for a disaster | That corp −1 |
| A corp solves a crisis the others couldn't | That corp +1 |
| A corp's secret agenda is exposed to the Quorum | That corp −1, or −2 if the colonists find out |
| The outriders deliver a deniable win for a corp | That corp +1 at the next milestone |
| A home board vetoes a corp's decision during the Long Line | That corp −1 |

Never let total Standing drift far from 10; when one corp gains, have another lose.

### How each corp cheats

Each corp has a lever in the Compact that it will push as soon as the lag allows. Let the players discover these one at a time.

| Corp | Lever | How it works |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weyland | **The captain** | The captain may act alone in an emergency, and the Quorum ratifies afterward. Weyland manufactures emergencies, and in the Dark it wants ties so the captain decides. |
| NBN | **The queue** | Every message home goes through NBN's comms. In the Long Line, NBN can hold a veto request for hours, timing it to land after the 72-hour window closes, or delay one corp's referrals while expediting another's. |
| Haas-Bioroid | **The minutes** | Bioroids staff the Quorum chamber and log every word, including what delegates say in the corridor afterward. Haas-Bioroid always knows how the vote will go before it happens. |
| Jinteki | **The proxies** | A clause lets a delegate who is incapacitated hand their vote to a medical proxy. Jinteki runs medical. A delegate who falls ill at the right moment hands Jinteki a second vote. |

### The dormant clause

Article 19 of the Compact, which nobody has read: if a majority of waking colonists sign a petition, the council seat gains Standing equal to that of the lowest corp. All four corps' lawyers know it exists. None of them will mention it.

Plant it in Ki (the saboteur has a marked-up copy, or the kid who wants to be an outrider finds it), and pay it off in Ketsu. It is the legal path to the colonists taking a seat at the table, and every corp will try to stop the petition, fake it, or buy it.

### Story beats by band

- **Tether.** Disputes are petty and the stakes are reputational: department squabbles, NBN's camera crew, the council election. Show the Quorum working, and let players see a home board slap down a delegate.
- **Long Line.** The first decision rushed through before a veto can arrive. The first NBN message that arrives suspiciously late. The outriders' first Finding, where whichever side they rule against becomes an enemy. A delegate falls ill and Jinteki votes twice.
- **The last message home.** Just before the gate, each delegate sends one final transmission to their board and receives sealed orders back. NBN reads all of them. Make this a set piece: the players may be hired to intercept one, protect one, or carry one.
- **Dark.** The Quorum has no one to answer to. Whoever leads in Standing at the crossing sets the agenda for Shō, and the corps' secret agendas start to break as described in the Campaign Arc.

### Connections to the Campaign Arc

- **The saboteur** knows what the final sealed orders say, which is part of why they want the ship stopped before the gate.
- **The outrider handler** steers which Findings the crew gets, to push Standing toward whoever is really paying them.
- **The town council** deal with Weyland in Shō can be Weyland offering to back the Article 19 petition in exchange for the survivors.
- **The NBN footage** of each Finding is how home and the colonists judge the outriders, and it may not show what actually happened.
